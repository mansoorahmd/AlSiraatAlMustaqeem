Research Gate Atlas

A map of how MQ Research Gate, a detective's study for the Qur'an, is built, and how access and monetization work. It is also our shared notebook: every section has a feedback thread, and the whole history is kept so we can settle changes here rather than in chat.

How to use this page. Type your name once (top right), then leave notes in the Notes & feedback box under any section. Entries are saved to this page and persist — I read them back and reply in the same threads, marked Claude. The Communication log at the end shows everything in one place.

The one rule to hold onto: features are role-based, resources are plan-based. A role says what you may do (publish, review, administer); a plan says what you may read (the corpus, the community, each translation and dictionary). Your own research stays on your machine and is always yours. Behind it, one constraint: you can only charge for what the server holds — which is why the corpus now lives on the research server.

01System map

Your research lives on your machine. The Qur'an corpus and the community live on the research server, and both the app and the AI read them from there — the app with your sign-in, the AI with a token that acts as you. Every read passes the same gates.

02The databases

Reference material on the cloud; your own work on your machine.

📖 The corpus cloud

The Book and everything known about its words — Postgres schema corpus.

  • Text in four scripts, per-word morphology, roots, ~10 classical lexicons, translations
  • Read-only; loaded from quran.db by npm run corpus:migrate and verified exact, row for row
  • Served at /corpus by the same code as the local server — one codebase, two drivers
  • Proven: corpus:parity compared 77,439 requests — every chapter, verse, root, word and search — all identical

✒️ research.db local

What you build on top — your earned understanding.

  • Cases & board, established meanings, trails, notes, indications, motifs
  • Read + write, self-migrating, the one irreplaceable file
  • Self-describing: a one-row owner table says whose work it is
  • The AI's proposals land here, tagged source='ai', for you to accept
Why this matters for money. While the corpus sat in a file on the reader's disk, it couldn't be charged for. On the research server it is a resource: the admin can gate it by plan — or make it free, or open it to anyone signed in — at any time. Your research stays local and is never gated.

03Features and resources

Everything gated falls into one of two kinds, and each kind has exactly one question.

Features role

What you may do. reader ‹ researcher ‹ moderator ‹ maintainer

  • Publish — submit notes, propose claims · researcher
  • Review — approve / object · moderator
  • Establish, invite, the Admin tab · maintainer
  • Connect an AI assistant (API tokens) · any account

Resources plan

What you may read. e.g. free (0) ‹ student (50) ‹ pro (100) ‹ scholar (200)

  • The Qur'an corpus — text, words, roots, dictionaries, search
  • The community — readings, dissent, ⚖ divergence, chips
  • Each translation — can need more than the corpus; below it, left out
  • Each dictionary — the same, per lexicon

The whole matrix

A resource's rule is a tier, free (any signed-in account) or public (no sign-in at all). Tiers are data the maintainer edits; a higher rank unlocks everything a lower one does. Role and plan are independent — a moderator on free can moderate; a tier called “admin” is only what's been paid for.

04The gates

Two middlewares, one per kind. requireRole(min) guards features; requireResource(kind) guards resources by reading the rule from resource_access — so changing who may read something is a row, not a deploy. Everything fails closed: an unknown tier never passes, and a lapsed plan counts as free.

Billing isn't wired yet — a maintainer grants a plan in the Admin tab (or npm run set-plan -w @alsiraat/remote -- you@example.org pro 30). That's the one seam a payment provider slots into later.

05Community: read live, never mirrored

Before, the group's readings were pulled down and mirrored into local derived_* tables so they'd work offline — which also meant they sat on the user's disk, forever, un-gateable. Now they're read live from the research server, behind requireResource('community'), and never stored locally.

06Publishing

Offering your work upstream — a note submission or a proposed claim — is a feature, so it asks one thing: your role. POST /submissions and POST /claims need researcher; review needs moderator; establishing needs maintainer. None of them asks for a plan any more.

What is plan-based is reading what others have published — that's the community resource (§05). So a researcher on free can still offer work; seeing the group's readings is what a plan buys.

07The MCP — reads the Book as you, writes to your research

The MCP lets an AI study the Book with you. Its corpus tools — read_ayah, study_root, trace_word, search_quran and the rest — now read the corpus from the research server, as you: it signs in with a personal API token, so it sees exactly what your plan allows. Its proposals still go into your local research.db, tagged source='ai', for you to accept in the app.

Connecting it

  • App → Account → Connect an AI assistant → Create token
  • The token is shown once, inside a ready-to-paste MCP config
  • Revoke it there any time — the AI is cut off at once

Its environment

  • REMOTE_URL — the research server (default http://localhost:8100)
  • REMOTE_TOKEN — the token
  • MQ_CORPUS=local — read quran.db instead (offline)
When it can't read, it says why in words the AI can pass on: no token → create one in the app; below the corpus tier → needs the pro plan; server down → set REMOTE_URL, or MQ_CORPUS=local. A test runs every corpus tool both ways — over HTTP and locally — and requires byte-identical answers.

08Admin — in the app, for maintainers

Signed in as a maintainer, an Admin tab appears beside Home, Read and Investigate. Every value reads as text with one Change; changes apply at once, and the server enforces all of it again.

Plan tiers

  • The ladder: rename, re-rank, add (e.g. student 50, scholar 200)
  • free is always rank 0; a tier held by someone or required by a resource can't be removed

Who can read

  • The corpus and the community: anyone, signed in, or a tier or higher
  • Each translation and each dictionary: its own tier, or same as the corpus

People

  • Everyone's role (features) and plan (resources), with an optional expiry in days
  • The last maintainer can't be demoted

Also scriptable

  • npm run access -- show | tier | corpus | community | translation | lexicon
  • npm run set-plan -w @alsiraat/remote -- email tier [days]
Heads-up for you. Your account is maintainer · free, and the corpus rule is pro — so today the app says Reading this needs the pro plan for you too. Fix it in Admin → People → Change → Pro, or Admin → Who can read → the corpus → Signed in. (Whether maintainers should bypass resource gates is an open question.)
