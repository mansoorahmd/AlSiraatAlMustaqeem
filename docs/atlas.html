Research Gate Atlas

A map of how MQ Research Gate, a detective's study for the Qur'an, is built, and how access and monetization work. It is also our shared notebook: every section has a feedback thread, and the whole history is kept so we can settle changes here rather than in chat.

How to use this page. Type your name once (top right), then leave notes in the Notes & feedback box under any section. Entries are saved to this page and persist — I read them back and reply in the same threads, marked Claude. The Communication log at the end shows everything in one place.

The one rule to hold onto: features are role-based, resources are plan-based — and your research is yours: private in your account until you publish it, and then seen only by the audience it names, once a reviewer approves it.

01System map

Everything lives on the research server: the Qur'an corpus, your research (private to you — row-level security) and the community. The app on your machine and the AI (through the MCP) read and write it as you — the app with your sign-in, the AI with a token that acts as you. Every request passes the same gates.

02The databases

All on the research server, in one Postgres.

📖 The corpus plan-gated

The Book and everything known about its words — schema corpus.

  • Text in four scripts, morphology, roots, ~10 classical lexicons, translations
  • Loaded from quran.db and verified exact; served by the same code as the local server
  • Proven: 77,439 requests compared with the local copy — all identical

✒️ Your research private

What you build — your earned understanding. One shared schema, research, kept apart by row-level security.

  • Cases & board, established meanings, trails, notes, indications, motifs, comparisons
  • Every row carries your account; the database itself shows each request only its own user's rows — a draft is yours alone, staff included
  • Only here — nothing of it is kept on your computer; the server's nightly backup is its copy
  • The AI's proposals land here, tagged ai, for you to accept
Private unless published. Nothing in your research is visible to anyone else. Publishing makes a frozen copy for review; it becomes the group's only once approved — and even then only for the audience it names (§04).

03Features and resources

Everything gated falls into one of two kinds, and each kind has exactly one question.

Features role

What you may do. A ladder you edit: reader ‹ student ‹ researcher ‹ scholar ‹ moderator ‹ maintainer

  • Keep your own research · any account
  • Publish — submit notes, propose readings · the role you choose (researcher by default)
  • Review — approve / object · moderator (fixed)
  • Establish, invite, the Admin tab · maintainer (fixed)

Resources plan

What you may read. e.g. free (0) ‹ pro (100) ‹ …

  • The Qur'an corpus — text, words, roots, dictionaries, search
  • The community — readings, dissent, ⚖ divergence, chips
  • Each translation, each dictionary — can need more than the corpus

The whole matrix

Roles and plans are independent: a scholar on free can do what scholars do, but reads only what free reaches; a paying reader reads more but can't publish until their role allows it.

04The gates

Two middlewares, one per kind. requireRole(min) guards features; requireResource(kind) guards resources by reading the rule from resource_access — so changing who may read something is a row, not a deploy. Everything fails closed: an unknown tier never passes, and a lapsed plan counts as free.

Billing isn't wired yet — a maintainer grants a plan in the Admin tab (or npm run set-plan -w @alsiraat/remote -- you@example.org pro 30). That's the one seam a payment provider slots into later.

05Community: read live, never mirrored

Before, the group's readings were pulled down and mirrored into local derived_* tables so they'd work offline — which also meant they sat on the user's disk, forever, un-gateable. Now they're read live from the research server, behind requireResource('community'), and never stored locally.

06Publishing — and who sees it

Publishing is a feature: you need the role the maintainer set for it (researcher by default; it could be student). Every published result then carries an audience: at least a role and at least a plan — e.g. scholars and above, on Pro.

Who decides

  • The author proposes the audience when publishing
  • The reviewer confirms or changes it when approving
  • Nothing is visible before approval — except to its author and the reviewers

Where it applies

  • Every community read: readings, the group's reading, dissents, chips, ⚖ divergence, the pull
  • Below the audience, a result simply isn't there for you
  • Authors always see their own; moderators and maintainers see everything

07The MCP — studies as you, proposes into your research

The MCP lets an AI study the Book with you. It reads the corpus and your research from the research server as you (a personal API token), and writes only proposals into your research — tagged ai, for you to accept in the app.

The boundary — enforced by the server

  • May add notes, indications, motifs, cases, and its own items on your cases
  • Never overwrites or deletes your records; never sets your primary gloss
  • Never touches your verdicts, status or established meanings; can't publish or change settings
  • A token can't administer or mint tokens — revoke it to cut the AI off

Its environment

  • REMOTE_URL — the research server
  • REMOTE_TOKEN — from Account → Connect an AI assistant
  • MQ_CORPUS=local — read quran.db instead (offline); your research is always your account's
Belt and braces. The MCP keeps its own guard, and the server applies the same rules to every token request — so they hold even if something calls the API directly. A test runs the real tools against the cloud research and checks both.

08Admin — in the app, for maintainers

Signed in as a maintainer, an Admin tab appears. Every value reads as text with one Change; changes apply at once, and the server enforces all of it again.

Roles

  • The ladder: add learner rungs (student, scholar, …) between reader and moderator, rename, re-rank, remove
  • What each role may do: the role publishing needs

Plan tiers · Who can read

  • The plan ladder; the corpus and the community: anyone / signed in / a tier
  • Each translation and dictionary: its own tier, or the corpus's

People

  • Everyone's role (standing) and plan (paid), with an optional expiry
  • The last maintainer can't be demoted

Also scriptable

  • npm run access -- …, npm run set-plan -- …
  • On the VPS: docker compose run --rm remote access show
Heads-up for you. Your research starts empty in your account — the old research.db files are gone (in the Recycle Bin). And your account is maintainer · free while the corpus rule is pro — grant yourself Pro in Admin → People, or open the corpus in Who can read.
